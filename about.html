About the Body Atlas

Content version 0.3.0 · updated 12 November 2026 · translated from the French original (Atlas du corps)

Emergency: call 112 (Europe; 15 in France), 999 (UK), 911 (US and Canada) or your local emergency number. Deaf or hard of hearing in France: 114 (text, video, chat).

What the atlas is for, and what it is not for

The atlas shows, on an anatomical model, where common joint and back pains are located, which structures they involve, and which exercises are commonly suggested in rehabilitation. It is general information, to help you understand, and to prepare for or follow up on a consultation.

It does not diagnose, does not assess your situation, does not choose a treatment for you and does not replace an examination by a doctor or a physiotherapist. The “area” and “moment” filters simply look for words in the cards: the same pain can have other causes that are not in the atlas. It is intended for adults.

When to seek help without waiting

Call emergency services if:

  • an arm or a leg suddenly loses strength; you have difficulty passing urine or holding it in; feeling is reduced between the legs or around the anus;
  • the pain comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, or a sudden, unusual headache;
  • after a fall or a blow: severe pain, deformity, or being unable to put weight on the leg, especially in an older person;
  • a joint becomes red, hot and swollen with fever;
  • a calf is swollen, red and painful.

See a doctor promptly (your doctor, or an out-of-hours service) if:

  • the pain wakes you at night, comes with fever or unexplained weight loss, or occurs in someone who has or has had cancer;
  • it gets worse despite relative rest, or does not improve within 4 to 6 weeks;
  • in a child or teenager: any limp, or hip, thigh or knee pain without an obvious cause. The atlas exercises are not designed for them.

How the cards are written

The code and the texts — cards, exercises, warning signs — were written with Claude, an artificial intelligence model by Anthropic, from general knowledge, then checked and published by the site’s publisher, who is not a health professional. To date, no card has been reviewed by a health professional. Each card shows its review status and the date it was last changed. The whole-body figures cite their source; the studies do not all measure the same thing, which is why the list groups them by order of magnitude instead of ranking them one by one. Within each joint, the order of the pains is an estimate without a published figure.

The atlas was written in French. This English version is a translation made with the same AI; if the two differ, the French version prevails. Emergency numbers and some advice reflect the French healthcare system.

Review follows a public policy: a source for every medical change, and a professional’s agreement to remove or soften a warning sign. The history of every change is public on GitHub.

Videos and links

The “video” links open YouTube: either a search, whose results are not checked, or a specific video chosen by a contributor. If a video does not match the written instructions, follow the written instructions. Linked sites have their own rules.

Reporting a mistake

Every card has a “Suggest a correction” link. Reports that could put someone in danger are reviewed within 7 days; if they cannot be fixed in time, the item is removed. The forms are public: do not describe your own medical situation in them, we cannot give personal advice. Reports in English are welcome.

Personal data

The atlas creates no account, sets no cookie, uses no analytics or advertising, and sends your choices nowhere: the areas and filters you select stay in the page and disappear when you close it. Only your language choice (French or English) is kept in your browser (local storage) for your next visit; it is never transmitted.

Hosting: the site is served by GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address and technical information about your browser in order to send you the pages; GitHub states that it keeps visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes (GitHub Pages documentation, GitHub privacy statement).

Third-party resources: none. Fonts and the 3D library are served by the site itself.

External links: nothing is sent to YouTube or to the source websites until you click.

Reports: the correction forms are on GitHub, public, and require a GitHub account.

For any question: private report on GitHub. You can also lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the CNIL, or your own national authority.

Accessibility

This site, made by a private individual, is not subject to a legal accessibility obligation and has not been audited. It aims to remain usable with a keyboard and a screen reader: the list of pains and the cards are text. The 3D model is not accessible; the essentials of what it shows are repeated in the cards.
